Abstract: Flat bands are usually associated with periodic lattice geometries, where destructive interference or chiral-symmetry constraints produce macroscopically degenerate eigenstates. Here we show that flat bands arise generically in a broad class of nonperiodic random networks through a simple edge-inflation procedure. Starting from random regular, Voronoi tessellation, Delaunay triangulation, scale-free, small-world, and Erdös-Rényi seed graphs, each edge is replaced by a finite one-dimensional tight-binding chain, either with fixed length $L$ or with random lengths of mean $\bar L$. Although the original random graphs typically have adjacency matrices of full rank and therefore few or no zero eigenstates, their inflated counterparts exhibit extensive flat bands. The flat-band energies are determined by the eigenstates residing on the chains or, more generally, by the eigenvalues of identical loopless motifs replacing the seed bonds. The corresponding eigenstates are states supported on subsets of the inflated chains formed by destructive interference at the original seed vertices. For zero-energy flat bands, we relate the degeneracy to the graph nullity $η(G)$ and the maximum matching number $ν(G)$. The numerical results support the correspondence $η(G)=N-2ν(G)$, up to small deviations, across the random graph ensembles studied. The fraction of degenerate zero-energy states in inflated random graphs is controlled primarily by the average degree of the seed graph. We further show that the flat bands exhibit characteristic robustness to disorder: disorder on the seed sites and random magnetic flux leave the flat bands essentially unchanged, while bond disorder and disorder on the chain or motif sites broaden them. These results show that flat bands do not require crystalline order, but can emerge as robust consequences of local graph inflation and motif substitution.
